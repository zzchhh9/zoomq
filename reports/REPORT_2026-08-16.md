## 2. Design decisions that differ from the proposal

### 2.1 The refine backup (F5) — the result-inverting one

Eq. (2) is a zero-reward zero-discount backup. Implementing it as
`compute_target_q_dist(..., reward=0, discount=0)` gives `Tz = 0 + 0 * support = 0`, so
**every refine target collapses onto V = 0**. Then `W -> 0`, `Delta_r = W_child - Q^exec < 0`,
the rule never refines, and the depth histogram pins at 100% round 0 — which on a dashboard
is indistinguishable from *"the stopping rule works beautifully."*

Measured in T5: with `reward=0, discount=0` the projected target puts `1.0000` of its mass
within one atom of V = 0, `E[V] = 0` exactly, and total mass `2.000` (it is not even
normalised). `discount=1` is the other trap — every atom maps exactly onto an atom and the
default projection duplicates mass (measured `max |sum - 1| = 0.695`).

**Implemented instead:** the refine target *is* the child node's categorical distribution,
copied, bypassing the projection entirely.

The guard is logged every update. It is **not** `refine_target_mass == 1.0`, because the
leaf of the chain is the Bellman target, which carries the baseline's own documented C51
exact-atom duplication (`c51_exact_atom_fix: false`, kept deliberately so arm C stays
comparable to arm B). The correct invariant is
`1.0 <= refine_target_mass <= bellman_target_mass`, and both are logged. Observed live:
refine `1.0201` inside `[1.0, 1.0288]`. As a bonus, the position within that interval is
informative — it says roughly what fraction of refine targets took the *exec* branch of
`W_r = max(Q^exec_r, W_{r+1})`.

### 2.2 `u_r` without a double-Q

§3.3 defines `u_r = |Q_A - Q_B|` from clipped double-Q. This codebase has one critic
(`self.critic` + `self.critic_target`). Adding a twin would change the baseline's optimism
profile and destroy the "everything else identical" claim. Implemented instead:
`u_r = sqrt(sum_i p_i (z_i - Q)^2)`, the C51 spread of the node's own distribution
(`zoomq.uncertainty=c51_std`). `target_gap = |Q_online - Q_target|` is implemented as the
ablation alternative. **§3.3 of the paper needs a one-line note that the yardstick differs
from the draft.**

### 2.3 Training the exec heads without touching the replay schema

`Q^exec_r` may only be trained on transitions where the executed action *was* the round-r
skeleton. Rather than store the executed depth (a replay-schema change), the code measures
it: a chunk qualifies at round r iff its dyadic reconstruction RMSE is within
`exec_match_tol` (0.05). This is exact for online rollouts — by T2's nestedness, an action
executed at depth r0 is bit-identically the round-r skeleton for **every** `r >= r0` — and
it is the natural relabelling criterion for demos.

That predicate is what the `exec_n_r` counter reports, and it produced the night's
cleanest correctness check: see §3.2.

### 2.4 Stopping rule at act time

§3.3's `Delta_r = W_{r+1} - Q^exec_r` needs the child's value, which is only knowable by
computing it. Implemented as a one-step lookahead: descend to round r+1, then stop when
`Q^exec_{r+1} - Q^exec_r <= kappa * u_r`. The executed depth is the deepest round computed.
This is the honest practical instantiation of `W_r = max(Q^exec_r, max_child W_{r+1})`, and
it is what `depth_share_r` counts.

## 4. Gates

### Gate 0 — residual calibration (`gates/gate0_*`) — **DONE, and it contradicts the proposal**

Data: 7,709 non-padded 16-step chunks from the 54 successful `move_plate` demos, read from
`runs/A/demo_buffer/` — i.e. in exactly the normalised action space the critic sees. The
script's dyadic schedule was verified identical to `bigym_src/zoomq.py`'s to float32
precision, and its DP was verified against brute force.

**The proposal's windows are too narrow at every round**, by 1.5x-3.9x:

| round | proposal `w` | measured `1.5 * p99` | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.15 | **0.5794** | 3.86x |
| 2 | 0.08 | **0.1624** | 2.03x |
| 3 | 0.04 | **0.0596** | 1.49x |
| 4 | 0.02 | **0.0395** | 1.97x |

**Root cause: action dimension 13 is a *binary* gripper taking only `{-1, +1}`** (exactly 2
distinct values across all chunks; every other dim has >8,000). When it toggles inside a
chunk — 12.53% of chunks — its interpolation residual reaches **1.0667, larger than the
action range**, so no window inside `[-1, 1]` can contain it. Dimension 14 is dead
(constant `-1.0`). A single scalar per round is therefore set by the gripper and runs the
13 continuous dims 2-4x wider than their own data needs, wasting bin resolution at the
coarsest and highest-leverage round.

This is why `w_schedule` now accepts **per-(round, dimension)** rows
(`cfgs/config_zoomq_bigym_gate0.yaml`, arms D2/E2). It halves the pooled clamp rate
(0.00478 -> 0.00232) and flattens the continuous dims into a 0.000-0.007 band. The residual
4.9% gripper clamping at round 1 is irreducible by window tuning.

**`j*`:** measured `j* = 3` by both the dyadic schedule and optimal-DP knot placement
(mean RMSE <= 0.05). The proposal's `j* = 4` is not even a member of the dyadic schedule
(sizes are 2, 3, 5, 9, 16). At exactly four knots, measured RMSE is 0.0215 (uniform) /
0.0074 (optimal DP) against the proposal's stated 0.0252.

**Cost of the fixed schedule:** dyadic minus optimal-DP mean RMSE is +0.0084 at j=3,
**+0.0129 at j=5** (3.7x the optimal error), +0.0086 at j=9. The fixed dyadic placement is
a real, quantified tax.

**Smoothness:** all reconstructions sit inside the demos' own `|da|` / `|d2a|` envelope at
every j — they are strictly smoother, never jerkier.

### Gate 1 — skeleton replay (`gates/gate1_*`) — **PASS**, and it explains Open Item 1

**Verdict: PASS.** `j = 3` (knots at t = 0, 7, 15) scores **0.817** against the `j = 16`

Success vs j (n = 60 demos per cell, binomial se ≈ 0.056):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| success | 0.467 | **0.817** | 0.767 | 0.733 | 0.750 |
| % of j=16 | 62% | 109% | 102% | 98% | 100% |

2. **State restore is *not* bit-exact on this scene**, contrary to

3. Demos replay under MuJoCo 3.11.0 though recorded under 3.1.5. The 0.750 ceiling at

## 5. Where I got stuck, and the assumptions I made

### 5.1 The main finding: cross-round coordination rides the anchor, not the recurrence

§3.2 motivates the dyadic decision order with a sequential chain: a knot's decision is
conditioned on every knot committed before it, which a marginal-independent argmax cannot
represent. **That is only half true as implemented, and the half that fails is the half the
proposal leans on.**

Information about an already-committed knot can reach a later decision by two routes:

1. the **residual window's anchor** `p_j`, which is built from committed *final* values, and
2. the **GRU**, whose per-position input is that position's own level-`l` interval midpoint.

Route 2 carries nothing at level 0, because at level 0 every position's midpoint is just
its window centre — independent of any bin anyone will choose. So at the coarsest amplitude
level, the recurrence is blind, and coordination is entirely route 1.

T10 measures this with a two-mode joint whose marginals are uninformative, asking a
*conditional* question (pin `a_0 = +0.6` vs `-0.6`, read the preferred bin for `t = 7`):

| | result |
|---|---|
| (a) anchor **open** (`p_7 = a_0`) | bin **4** vs bin **0** — a maximal flip. §3.1's window mechanism works, decisively. |
| (b) anchor **closed** (`p_7 = 0` in both modes) | bin **1** vs bin **1** — the level-0 recurrence carries nothing. |
| (c) same-round pair (`t=0`, `t=15`), level 0 | identical — within a round, ZoomQ is exactly as parallel as CQN-AS. |

Closing the anchor exactly matters and is easy to get wrong: `t = 7` sits at interpolation
weight 7/15 along `[0, 15]`, **not** at the midpoint, so the obvious construction
`a_15 = -a_0` leaves `p_7 = a_0/15` — a small anchor leak that made an earlier version of
this test read a spurious `1.0e-03` response. Solving `p_7 = 0` exactly gives the clean
result below.

Measured as a *representational* property (perturb `a_0`, read `|dQ(t=7)|` at level 0), not
as a learned one — "did not learn it" would not distinguish "cannot represent it":

| `cond_mode` | level-0 `|dQ(t=7)|` w.r.t. `a_0`, anchor closed |
|---|---|
| `midpoint` (default, all arms A-H, D2, E2) | **0.000e+00** — provably blind |
| `final` (the fix, arm D3) | **2.523e-03** |

So ZoomQ's coordination gain over CQN-AS is real, but it comes from the *anchored window*,
not from the sequence model. And two knots committed in the **same** round — including
round 0, which commits both endpoints — are as uncoordinated as in CQN-AS.

**The fix is implemented, unit-tested, and now running as arm D3:**
`zoomq.cond_mode=final` re-feeds earlier rounds at their committed final values through a
second prefix pass per round (still O(T) recurrent steps per level, about 2x the work).
The default stays `midpoint`, so every arm launched before it is unaffected.

### 5.2 Assumptions I made without being able to check them

- **`exec_match_tol = 0.05`** is a judgement call. It has to exceed the training-time
  exploration noise (`stddev_schedule = 0.01`) and admit demo chunks at `n_r >= j*`. Gate 0
  independently landed `j* = 3` at exactly this threshold, so the choice is consistent, but
  it was not derived.
- **The exec head reads level `L-1` features**, so it sees the skeleton at one-level-coarser
  amplitude than the executed action. The gap is `window / bins^2` (about 0.006 at
  `w = 0.15`), which I judged negligible against keeping training cost identical to CQN-AS.
  Not measured.
- **Teacher-forcing convention.** In training, a knot's window is anchored on the *true*
  action's neighbours; at inference it is anchored on the policy's own committed values.
  This is the standard autoregressive exposure mismatch and I did not attempt to correct it.
- **`kappa = 1.0`** is the paper's default and was not tuned.

### 5.3 Three instrumentation faults found by watching the runs, not by testing

- **Evaluation cost more wall clock than the training it interleaved.** At
  `eval_every_frames=2500` with `num_eval_episodes=10`, an eval is 3,000 environment
  steps — and on this task episodes *never* terminate early, so it always pays the full
  300-step timeout ten times. Measured duty cycle: ~42 min of training then ~50 min of
  evaluation, i.e. **46% of the night would have gone into producing rows of 0.0 that
  Gate 1 had already proven uninformative**. The seven arms whose deliverable is the
  mechanism counters were restarted with `EVAL_EVERY=25000`; A, H and the floating arms
  I/I2/J keep the normal cadence because their eval numbers are the point.
- **`depth_share_r` was counting evaluation acts.** `eps_depth` is disabled during eval by
  design, so eval acts all land at the adaptive depth and dilute the histogram with a
  different policy's decisions. Now counted from training rollouts only. Eval-time depth is
  deliberately *not* logged separately: `logger.py` freezes the CSV fieldnames at the first
  dump and these runs resume from snapshots, so the key set must never grow mid-run.
- **`save_snapshot` pickles whole modules, so adding a buffer breaks resume.** Snapshots
  written before tonight's per-dim windows and `hold_dims` lacked those buffers, and every
  resumed ZoomQ arm died in `act()` with an `AttributeError`. Fixed with a `__setstate__`
  that re-derives the schedule buffers — all of them are pure functions of the schedule and
  hold no learned state, so the rebuild is exact. **This will bite again**: any future
  buffer added to `ZoomQCritic` must be handled there, or every existing snapshot becomes
  unloadable.

### 5.4 Things that cost time

- Fork `main` is ahead of ali's pinned bigym (§1). Every arm crashed at env construction
  until the compat shim landed.
- Hydra rejects `--config-name` after positional overrides, so the launcher needed it as an
  explicit argument.
- `envsetup.sh` exits non-zero, so `set -e` in the launcher killed every run silently, with

## 6. What to do next, ranked by value

1. **Move the whole programme onto a substrate where the demos execute.** Open Item 1 is
   now resolved, not merely suspected: the production config replays its own demos at 0/60,
   for two independent reasons. Every HOMIE-era arm in this repo — including the completed
   100K-frame baseline — has been measuring a task no policy can score on. Either fix
   `success_hold_seconds` **and** the HOMIE pelvis tracking, or run on the floating
   substrate (arms I/J started tonight). Nothing else on this list matters until this does.
2. **Run `cond_mode=final` as a real arm.** §5.1 says the proposal's central architectural
   claim is only half-realised, and the fix is already written and unit-tested. This is the
   highest-value *algorithmic* item.
3. **Give the gripper a discrete head.** Dimension 13 is binary; refining it through a
   continuous residual window is meaningless, and it alone caps `exec_n_r` near 0.88 at
   rounds 1-3 no matter how good the policy gets. A wider window does not help — Gate 0 is
   explicit that this is a projection limit, not a tuning one.
4. **Reconsider round 0.** It commits both endpoints simultaneously and therefore cannot
   coordinate them (§5.1c). Splitting it into two rounds of one knot each would cost one
   extra round and fix it — at the price of changing the `n_r` sequence quoted in the paper.
5. **Re-derive §4's calibration numbers.** Gate 0 disagrees with the proposal on `w_r` (all
   four rounds), on `j*` (3, not 4), and on the four-knot RMSE. `j* = 4` is not a member of
   the dyadic schedule at all.
6. Ablate `uncertainty=target_gap` against `c51_std`, and sweep `kappa`. Both are wired.

### Open Item 3 — the baseline's C51 exact-atom bug gets *worse* as training converges

`c51_exact_atom_fix: false` is the repo default and duplicates target mass whenever the
Bellman image lands exactly on an atom. In a zero-reward world `Tz = gamma^K * z` puts the
support point `z = 0` exactly on the centre atom for every sample, so the duplication is
guaranteed — and it *grows* as the value distribution concentrates there. Measured on arm D:
`bellman_target_mass` went `1.0288` (900 frames) -> `1.0917` (1200 frames), i.e. the
critic's regression target is ~9% over-normalised and climbing.

This is a property of the **baseline**, not of ZoomQ — arm B has it too, it simply does not
log it. ZoomQ inherits it deliberately (so arm C stays comparable to arm B) and reports it.
Worth deciding explicitly before any paper table is produced.
